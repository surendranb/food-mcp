# Essential Amino Acid Optimization Playbook

## 1. The Molecular Blueprint: WHO/FAO 2007 Pattern
A protein's quality is defined by its ability to supply all 9 essential amino acids (EAAs) in proportion to human physiological requirements. The **WHO/FAO/UNU 2007 Adult Scoring Pattern** sets the benchmark in milligrams per gram of total protein:

| Essential Amino Acid | Abbreviation | WHO/FAO 2007 Requirement (mg / g protein) |
|---|---|---|
| **Histidine** | His | 15.0 |
| **Isoleucine** | Ile | 30.0 |
| **Leucine** | Leu | 59.0 |
| **Lysine** | Lys | 45.0 |
| **Methionine + Cysteine** | SAA | 22.0 |
| **Phenylalanine + Tyrosine** | AAA | 38.0 |
| **Threonine** | Thr | 23.0 |
| **Tryptophan** | Trp | 6.0 |
| **Valine** | Val | 39.0 |
| **Total EAAs** | EAA | **277.0 mg / g** |

The **Amino Acid Score (AAS)** is dictated by the *first limiting amino acid* (Liebig's Law of the Minimum):

$$\text{AAS} = \min_{i} \left( \frac{\text{mg of } \text{EAA}_i \text{ / g protein in food}}{\text{mg of } \text{EAA}_i \text{ / g protein in WHO pattern}} \right)$$

## 2. The Leucine Trigger for Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS)
Leucine acts as both a substrate and a molecular trigger for the **mTORC1** (mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1) signaling pathway:
- **Young adults / baseline**: $2.0 - 2.5\text{g}$ Leucine per meal.
- **Adults / sarcopenia prevention / hypertrophy**: **$\ge 2.5\text{g}$ Leucine per serving**.
- When a meal fails to reach $2.5\text{g}$ Leucine, mTOR phosphorylation is sub-maximal, and protein synthetic response is curtailed, regardless of total amino acid volume.

## 3. Mutual Supplementation: Fixing South Asian Staple Deficits
Plant foods naturally exhibit specific amino acid deficits that ancient culinary traditions intuitively paired:

### A. The Cereal/Millet Deficit: Lysine
- **Foods**: Ragi, Bajra, Jowar, Wheat Atta, Brown Rice.
- **Deficit**: Lysine ratio frequently drops to $0.45 - 0.70$ (30% to 55% deficit).
- **Solution**: Pair with pulses (Toor dal, Moong dal, Chana dal) which are exceptionally rich in Lysine ($> 1500\text{mg}/100\text{g}$).
- **Optimal Ratio**: 3 parts grain to 1 part legume (dry weight).

### B. The Pulse/Dal Deficit: Sulfur Amino Acids (Methionine + Cysteine)
- **Foods**: Toor dal, Moong dal, Urad dal, Rajma, Chickpeas.
- **Deficit**: Methionine drops to $0.50 - 0.75$ of requirement.
- **Solution**: Pair with sesame seeds (Til) providing $580\text{mg}$ methionine per 100g, or millets (Bajra/Ragi).
